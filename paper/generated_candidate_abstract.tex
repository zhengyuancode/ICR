When a tool used by a language-model agent fails, replacing one call can break
downstream interfaces or alter values needed by the remaining workflow. We
present Interface-Closed Recovery (ICR), which jointly selects replacements and
the smallest changed region that reconnects to unchanged execution. Its
recovery packets bind observed values and carry interface, function, and boundary-value
evidence. On a fixed read-only relation, functional dependencies and a
common-row witness certify candidates at the observed input without executing
them, enabling recovery advice backed by checkable evidence. Across
1,901 TaskBench graphs, ICR's exact optimizer matches exhaustive search and
finds 484 feasible repairs missed by a matched-information greedy baseline. On
PlanBench-XL, observed-input certification admits 774 additional shortest routes
that match native execution while avoiding 126 incorrect choices made by
type-only admission across 9,250 failed-tool--input cases. In a sealed 72-task
study, the complete ICR advice system succeeds on 58 tasks versus 39 for the native agent, a paired
gain of 26.4 percentage points. At 20 matched failure prefixes whose native-correct path
fails global certification, ICR restores the trusted target value within 12
turns in 19 cases, versus seven for the global-certificate arm.
